Publications

Selected publications

Selected publications from our lab. For a complete list of our papers, please visit the NCBI Bibliography.

Stomatin encapsulates aquaporin-1 and urea transporter-B in the erythrocyte membrane

Vallese F., Li H., Barazzuol L., Cali T., Clarke O. B.

Science Advances (2026). DOI / PMC

We solved structures of native stomatin complexes from red blood cells, showing how they form 'domes' to capture specific transport proteins.

High-resolution ab initio reconstruction enables cryo-EM structure determination of small particles

Kim K., Li H., Clarke O. B.

bioRxiv (2025). DOI

We developed HR-HAIR, a CryoSPARC pipeline that enables high-resolution 3D ab initio reconstruction and structure determination of small particles.

Structural identification of the RY12 domain of RyR1 as an ADP sensor and the target of the malignant hyperthermia therapeutic dantrolene

Kim K., Li H., Yuan Q., Melville Z., Zalk R., des Georges A., Frank J., Hendrickson W. A., Marks A. R., Clarke O. B.

bioRxiv (2024). DOI / PMC

We identified the binding sites for the malignant hyperthermia therapeutic drug dantrolene and the trigger 4CmC on RyR1, and identified the RY12 domain as an ADP sensor.

Architecture of the human erythrocyte ankyrin-1 complex

Vallese F., Kim K., Yen L. Y., Johnston J. D., Noble A. J., Cali T., Clarke O. B.

Nature Structural & Molecular Biology (2022). DOI / PMC

This structure shows how the massive ankyrin complex links the red blood cell membrane to the cytoskeleton to control cell shape.

High-resolution structure of the membrane-embedded skeletal muscle ryanodine receptor

Melville Z., Kim K., Clarke O. B., Marks A. R.

Structure (2022). DOI / PMC

We reconstructed the ryanodine receptor in lipid bilayers, proving that key binding sites are preserved in a native-like membrane.

Structural basis for gating and activation of RyR1

des Georges A., Clarke O. B., Zalk R., Yuan Q., Condon K. J., Grassucci R. A., Hendrickson W. A., Marks A. R., Frank J.

Cell (2016). DOI / PMC

We captured how activating ligands like calcium, ATP, and caffeine trigger structural changes that open the RyR1 channel pore.

Structure of a mammalian ryanodine receptor

Zalk R., Clarke O. B., des Georges A., Grassucci R. A., Reiken S., Mancia F., Hendrickson W. A., Frank J., Marks A. R.

Nature (2015). DOI / PMC

Our cryo-EM structure of the closed ryanodine receptor provided the first full architectural map of this massive channel.
