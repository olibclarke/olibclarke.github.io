Research

Mechanism from structure

We study the structural biology of membrane proteins and native macromolecular complexes. Our research combines biochemistry, single-particle cryo-EM, and computational methods to understand how these molecular machines function in their cellular environment.

Ion channels

Ryanodine receptor gating and allostery

The skeletal muscle ryanodine receptor (RyR1) is a massive ion channel that controls muscle contraction. We want to understand how it integrates signals from physiological activators (like calcium and nucleotides), inhibitors, and therapeutic drugs. By capturing RyR1 in different functional states, we map the long-range allosteric networks that govern channel gating.

Native membranes

Red blood cell membrane architecture

Red blood cells undergo extreme deformation as they travel through capillaries. We isolate intact, native supercomplexes directly from erythrocyte membranes to see how transporters, channels, and structural proteins link the lipid bilayer to the underlying spectrin cytoskeleton. These structures help explain how the membrane coordinates transport, maintains stability, and dictates cell shape.

Cryo-EM methods

Small-particle reconstruction and method development

While cryo-EM has revolutionized structural biology, small, flexible, or membrane-embedded proteins remain incredibly difficult to resolve. We develop new biochemical protocols and computational algorithms (such as advanced ab initio reconstruction workflows) to push the size limit of cryo-EM and solve structures that were previously considered impossible.
